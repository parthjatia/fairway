# Pitch (5 minutes) and judge Q&A

**Note for the presenter:** every number comes from a synthetic simulation. Say so early. Judges respect it. The line to land: **"Other launchpads tax everyone who's early. Fairway taxes snipers."**

**Open with the buyer's problem, not the fee curve:** "Before you buy a new Solana token, you cannot see who else bought it. Snipers take a large share of the first minute's supply and dump on the people who come after. That information is public onchain, so we built **Fairway Check**: paste a token and see who bought it early, how much the snipers hold, and whether they have already sold. Snipers get exposed everywhere, and taxed on Fairway." Then show one real `/check` report (live Stream), and move to Fairway Launch: "and when a token launches on Fairway, those same wallets pay the sniper fee."

**Lead with two numbers (200 simulated launches, vs a time-decay-only fee):** humans pay **53% less**, and the **sniper share of early supply falls from 49% to 27%**. If a judge says "but snipers pay about the same in total," the answer is that this is deterrence: known snipers face a 10% fee, so most of them don't buy, and the supply goes to humans instead.

## Script

**0:00 The hook (30 s).** *(Start with the buyer's problem above and a `/check` report, then:)* "Two numbers from 200 simulated launches: on Fairway, humans pay 53% less than with today's anti-sniper fee, and the snipers' share of early supply falls from 49% to 27%. Here's why. Every token launch on Solana goes the same way. In the first seconds, bots buy before any human can click. Then they sell into the crowd. In our simulation, with a normal fee, snipers take more than half of everything sold in the first five minutes."

**0:30 Today's fix, and why it's wrong (40 s).** "Launchpads fight this with a time-decay fee: 3% at the start, falling to normal. But it doesn't know who is buying. The human who buys one second after the sniper pays the same 3%. Humans pay about 200 bps in the window, and snipers just pay it too and still take half the supply. Other launchpads tax everyone who's early. Fairway taxes snipers."

**1:10 The mechanism (60 s).** Show `/launch/frog`. "On Fairway the launch fee depends on **who** and **when**. Each tier has its own curve over a 5-minute window: a known sniper pays 10% at slot 0, a new wallet 3%, the same as today, and a proven human 0.3%. The tier is an onchain account derived from the signer, so nobody can borrow a human's score, and a fresh wallet can never pay less than the standard curve."

**2:10 Where 'known sniper' comes from: QuickNode Streams (45 s).** "Snipers are repeat players. A QuickNode Stream runs our filter over Solana blocks and pulls every Pump.fun and PumpSwap buy and sell. Wallets that buy in the first seconds of launch after launch, and profit right after, score TOXIC, across at least three launches, so one lucky buy never counts. A keeper writes those scores onchain before our token even exists. After launch the program scores trades itself, against the pool's own TWAP, since a new token has no oracle."

**2:55 Demo (75 s).** `/launch`: walk the wizard (token, liquidity, window, review). Then `/launch/frog` and press play: the fee per tier falling, the buyers list filling with tier badges and fee paid, the sniper-share meter (Fairway vs time-decay), and **"Same buy, two wallets"**: at the opening slot, Rita's $200 buy costs her 60 cents and the same buy from a known sniper costs $20. `/backtest`: the launch study.

**4:10 Honest results and business model (35 s).** "Across 200 simulated launches, humans pay 53% less than under time-decay, in every launch, and the sniper share of early supply falls from 49% to 27%. Snipers pay about the same in total, and that's the point: it's deterrence. Known snipers see a 10% fee and stay out. Two honest limits: snipers on fresh wallets pay exactly the time-decay fee, because we can't know them, and creators earn less fee income than with time-decay, because that income came mostly from humans. The protocol earns a 0.05 SOL launch fee plus 15% of the sniper premium."

**4:45 Close (15 s).** "Anchor program with the launch curve and an onchain TWAP, two Streams filters, scoring, keeper and the full app, all working end to end offline. Next: devnet, verified Pump.fun history, and launchpad partners whose graduating tokens land in Fairway pools."

## Launch questions

1. **How is this different from existing anti-sniper fees?** Existing ones are a function of time only: everyone who is early pays the same high fee, the sniper and the human one second later. Fairway's fee is a function of time **and the buyer's tier**. The STANDARD curve (fresh wallets) *is* the usual time-decay curve, so we're never softer than today. Known snipers get a much steeper one (1000 bps), and proven humans a much gentler one (30 bps). In the sim, humans pay 94 bps instead of 200, and known snipers mostly stop buying.

2. **If snipers pay about the same in total, what's the point?** Deterrence. In the sim, all snipers pooled pay 1.01× what they pay under time-decay, because a known sniper faces 1000 bps at block 0 and mostly doesn't buy: known-sniper notional falls from about $25k to under $300 per launch. The ones who still buy pay 1.5× per dollar. The outcome that matters is who ends up holding the supply: the sniper share of early supply falls from 49% to 27%, and humans pay 53% less. Total sniper fees staying flat means the snipers we can't identify (fresh wallets) pay today's time-decay fee, which is the floor.

3. **Where does the price come from without arbitrage?** From the pool itself. A new token has no CEX price and no Pyth feed, so price discovery *is* the trading. For scoring, the program keeps a price × slots accumulator in the pool account (64 observations) and marks each trade against the 25-slot TWAP ending 75 slots (~30 s) after it. The reference is the post-trade price, so a buyer's own impact never counts as "information". It's tested: a trade into a pool that doesn't move afterwards scores exactly 0. Moving the TWAP to frame someone costs real price impact for ~10 s, and pays the launch fee both ways.

4. **Why would anyone launch here instead of Pump.fun?** Creators who want holders, not bots: a fairer distribution (sniper share 27% vs 49%), a lower entry price for their community, and a launch story ("snipers pay 10%, you pay 0.3%"). The honest cost: less fee income than a time-decay launch. The bigger path is not competing with Pump.fun but partnering: graduating tokens land in a Fairway pool with a launch curve (see "What's next").

5. **Can snipers just use fresh wallets?** Yes, and then they pay the STANDARD curve, which is exactly today's time-decay fee. Rotation removes the surcharge but can never earn a discount, because RETAIL needs ≥ 5 scored trades, ≥ 3 days of history and near-zero markout. Our worst-case simulation, where every sniper rotates, gives the same sniper outcome as time-decay, and humans with history still pay 94 instead of 200 bps. Fresh wallets also leave a funding trail: the Stream's funding check flags wallets funded by one source just before a launch (Q13).

6. **Why won't bots just go to other DEXes?** For a launch they can't: the token only trades in its launch pool for the first minutes, and that pool is ours. On liquid pairs, some will route elsewhere, and that's fine: the bots that leave are the flow that was costing LPs. The retail flow that stays pays a fair price.

7. **Where does the liquidity come from?** At launch, from the creator, exactly as today: they seed the pool (the wizard does mint + pool + liquidity + `configure_launch` in three transactions). After the window it's a normal constant-product pool where anyone can LP and earn fees. Launchpad partnerships would bring graduating tokens' liquidity directly.

8. **What does "known sniper" mean exactly?** A wallet whose early buys (within 25 slots of a launch) across ≥ 3 different Pump.fun launches have a shrunk markout score ≥ 500 bps: the price was much higher 30 s after it bought, consistently. Shrinkage is K = 5 SOL of prior notional. It's computed offchain from Stream data, written onchain by the keeper and expires (TTL) unless refreshed.

9. **Couldn't you wrongly label a human TOXIC?** A human would have to buy in the first ~10 s of three or more launches that then pumped. That is sniping behaviour, whatever is behind it. The damage is bounded: a higher fee in the window, never lost funds. Labels expire, and onchain-verified scores take over once the wallet trades on Fairway.

10. **Is the Pump.fun data real?** The filter and scorer are real, tested code, but tested on synthetic `getBlock`-shaped blocks. The Pump.fun and PumpSwap program IDs were verified onchain on 2026-11-19, but no live Stream has run yet. The filter matches on those program IDs only, so an unexpected transaction shape is skipped rather than labelled.

11. **How does the protocol make money?** A 0.05 SOL launch fee per launch (paid to the treasury by non-admin creators in `configure_launch`), plus 15% of the toxic premium (the TOXIC fee above STANDARD) on every pool. Next: licensing scores via `getFeeTier` to other DEXes, launchpads and wallets.

12. **Doesn't this help memecoin scams?** Fairway changes who pays the launch fee. It doesn't vet tokens, and a fair distribution doesn't make a token a good investment. A creator who snipes their own launch from fresh wallets pays the STANDARD curve like any other fresh wallet.

13. **What about bundlers?** A bundler funds 3–8 fresh wallets from one source just before the launch. The same QuickNode Stream that reads Pump.fun trades also extracts those fundings. Two or more early buyers funded by one source within ~10 minutes before the launch are a bundle, and their wallets pay at least STANDARD. A funder that does it in two launches is a known bundler: every new wallet it funds afterwards is flagged before it buys and pays the TOXIC curve. In the sim (assuming 60% of bundles come from a known bundler), the bundle share of early supply falls from 20% to 7.5%. Honest limits: one hop only (an exchange withdrawal breaks the link), and without detection Fairway actually leaves *more* room for bundles than time-decay, because known snipers stay out.

14. **Why Solana?** 400 ms slots give a precise clock for "the first seconds" and for the fee curve. Fast cheap transactions make per-launch scoring and batched score writes (10 per tx) practical. Pump.fun makes Solana the place where launch sniping happens, so the history we need is onchain there.

15. **Why Streams vs. polling RPC?** Pump.fun produces thousands of launches a day. Polling every new bonding curve with `getSignaturesForAddress` is impossible to keep up with. A Block stream with a server-side filter that matches the program IDs gets every launch trade, backfill and live through one pipeline, and only a few KB leave QuickNode per block.

## Engine and liquid-pair questions (SOL/USDC, secondary evidence)

16. **Can't bots just use new wallets (liquid pair)?** They get STANDARD (30 bps), the same as a flat-30 pool. Rotation removes only the *surcharge*. In our worst-case simulation arbs keep their full profit and the pool behaves exactly like a flat 30 bps pool (no premium exists, so nothing is rebated and the protocol earns nothing). That is the floor, not a failure.

17. **Doesn't a higher bot fee make the pool price staler?** Yes. p95 deviation from the CEX goes from 32 bps to about 80 bps at the default 80 bps toxic fee (+67 bps at 100 bps, +163 at 200 bps). The default setting is chosen with a +50 bps p95 bound. Retail's execution cost already includes the effect, which is why retail still comes out ahead, but a very stale pool price is a risk for anything that reads it as an oracle.

18. **Who runs the oracle?** For trades on Fairway, nobody: the program computes the score itself. Every swap is queued in the trader's PDA, and after 75 slots (about 30 s) anyone can call `settle_markout`, which reads Pyth and applies the same markout, shrinkage and thresholds onchain. Once a wallet has 1,000 USDC and 5 trades of settled flow, that onchain tier wins and the keeper can't change it. The keeper only bootstraps new wallets from their history on other venues (via QuickNode Streams). Even there, its power is limited to choosing a tier, scores expire (TTL), and the inputs are public.

19. **Why Solana?** Fast slots make LVR frequent, which makes the problem worse. Cheap batched writes (10 scores per transaction), PDAs let us key the fee tier to the signer cheaply, and QuickNode Streams makes the Solana history queryable.

20. **Why Streams vs. polling RPC?** Backfill plus live in one pipeline, server-side filtering (KBs instead of block-sized payloads), ordered delivery with retries, and one filter that works on any AMM through vault deltas.

21. **How is this different from dynamic volatility fees or Uniswap v4 hooks?** Volatility fees charge everyone more in volatile markets, including retail. Hooks are a mechanism where one can implement it, but need an information signal. Fairway's contribution is the *signal*: a wallet-level, markout-based score kept fresh from a data pipeline, with an onchain check that ties it to the signer.

22. **Does the simulation show LPs make more than a flat 30 bps pool?** No, and we don't claim it. The default (retail and standard 30 bps, toxic 80, 10% of the toxic premium rebated to retail, protocol 15% of the premium) cuts arbitrage profit by 73% (LVR −73%, LP-neutral within noise) and keeps retail cost at or below a flat 30 pool (−1.0 ± 0.7 bps). LP PnL vs flat 30 is +$15 ± 33/day before the protocol share (within noise) and −$56 ± 31/day after it, on a $3M pool, from 16 paired seeds. We swept toxic 40–100 bps, rebate 0–50% and five protocol-share options for a setting that beats flat 30 beyond the noise band; none does, because the fees arbitrageurs used to pay shrink along with their profit. A 150–200 bps toxic fee looks better (+$154 to +$213/day before the protocol share) but is still inside its noise band and makes the pool 116–163 bps staler at p95, so it is not the default. If retail volume responds to fees (elasticity 0.5–1, an assumption) the result is still within noise. What you get is a large cut in LP losses to bots, retail never overpaying, and a small, measured cost to LPs. The earlier 5 / 30 / 80 design is kept as a variant, and it costs LPs about $450/day.

23. **What stops a "sleeper" wallet that builds a clean history, then trades informed?** TTL and frequent refresh, TOXIC writes first, and the shrinkage prior. It's not solved. Notional caps for RETAIL are future work. We list it in RISKS.md.

24. **What about aggregators like Jupiter?** The tier follows the signer. If the router signs as the user, it works. A router that signs with its own account collapses many users into one wallet, which we haven't tested.

25. **Isn't labeling wallets a privacy or fairness problem?** Scores use only public data, and the explorer shows the exact inputs. There are regulatory questions about differential pricing that a production deployment would need to review. We flag it in RISKS.md.

26. **How do you measure markout without a CEX price?** Fixture mode uses the simulated CEX path. Live mode uses the pool-implied mid from the stream itself (last trade). A Pyth provider is stubbed behind the same interface for a later upgrade.

27. **Why K = 2,000?** It's a prior of $2k notional at markout 0. It means a wallet needs a few thousand dollars of volume before its score is near its raw average. It is configurable.

28. **What if the oracle goes down?** Bootstrap scores go stale after `score_ttl_slots`, and those wallets pay STANDARD. Onchain verified scores don't depend on the keeper and don't expire. If Pyth is stale or too uncertain, `settle_markout` refuses to settle, trades stay pending (then expire unscored) and tiers just stop changing. The pool keeps working either way.

29. **Is the program secure?** It's unaudited. Math is u128 checked, rounding in the pool's favor, with the k-never-decreases property tested. The wrong-signer, stale-score, slippage and unauthorized-write tests pass. Don't use real funds.

30. **What's real and what's simulated?** Real: the program, the scoring, the parser and filter (tested against getBlock-shaped fixtures), the ingest, keeper and the SDK. Simulated: all market data and the backtest, and the UI says so. Not done: a live Stream, a devnet deployment. Those are the human's final steps (SETUP_REMAINING.md).

31. **How does the protocol make money?** `protocol_fee_bps` takes 15% of the **toxic premium only**: the part of a TOXIC swap's fee above what STANDARD would pay. That is about $72/day on the simulated $3M pool, paid entirely by flagged bots, and nothing from retail or standard fees. (We tested 15% of all fees too: it would cost LPs about $215/day, so it is not the default.) The share is booked per pool, excluded from LP reserves, and claimed by anyone into the treasury PDA. Another 10% of the premium goes back to retail as the bot-tax rebate, hard-capped by the premium collected. The next revenue line is licensing: other DEXes price flow with our scores (`getFeeTier`), through an API, an SLA, or a revenue share. That second part is not built. Honest caveat: premium-only revenue is small and disappears if bots rotate wallets (then LPs just have a plain 30 bps pool).

32. **Are bots banned? Doesn't this hurt market makers?** No. The fee depends on measured markout, not on whether the signer is a program. A market maker or hedging bot with no informed edge earns RETAIL pricing and a "Verified fair flow" badge. Only flow that consistently moves the price its way pays the TOXIC fee.

33. **Who pays for the score account?** Each wallet's first swap on a scored pool creates its `TraderScore` PDA, about 0.0023 SOL of rent. The protocol treasury pays it while it holds more SOL than its own rent-exempt minimum plus that rent, and the account is flagged so closing it refunds the treasury. If the treasury is empty, the trader pays. Sponsoring is an onboarding cost the protocol covers from its fee revenue, and a drained treasury just falls back to trader-pays.

34. **Why a second, memecoin pool?** Because it shows the mechanism where it bites: pumps and dumps give snipers a large markout, so they are scored TOXIC after 5–9 settled trades in the fixtures, while the same sniper stays STANDARD after 3 trades on SOL/USDC. It is a test mint with synthetic data, and the real BONK has a Pyth feed that a test mint does not, so live, that pool would have no oracle until one is configured.

35. **Isn't publishing "TOXIC" wallet labels risky, and what does "Low risk" really mean?** The label is a score with its inputs shown: a wallet bought early in at least 3 launches and the price rose after it bought. It is pseudonymous, never calls anyone a bot or a scammer, and one lucky buy cannot trigger it. "Low risk" only means few *known* snipers bought early. A sniper on a fresh wallet looks like any new wallet, so Check understates them, and the launch page shows the all-bots meter next to it. The thresholds (30% High, 10% Medium) are public and have not been calibrated on real launches yet. It is evidence for a buyer, not a verdict.
36. **Why give Check away for free?** It is the front door. Check makes the problem visible to the buyers who feel it, with no wallet and no signup, and every report points at the fix: launch on Fairway, where the same wallets pay the sniper fee. The data behind it (QuickNode Streams and the sniper scores) is the same data the fee tiers use, so Check costs us nothing extra to run.
